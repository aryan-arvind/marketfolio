\begin{abstract}
Predictive modeling in financial time series frequently suffers from methodological flaws, notably the misapplication of regression algorithms to non-stationary asset returns under Eugene Fama's Weak-Form Efficient Market Hypothesis, alongside pervasive lookahead bias caused by indiscriminate temporal data shuffling. In this paper, we present \textbf{MarketFolio}, an academically grounded quantitative analytics and relational deep learning framework designed specifically for financial tail-risk detection, regime transitions, and inter-market systemic shock transmission. Grounded in advanced machine learning (ATML) paradigms, MarketFolio introduces a tripartite neural architecture evaluated under zero-leakage constraints: (1) a multi-channel \textbf{Variational Autoencoder (VAE)} that captures non-linear covariance manifolds to identify structural anomalies via reconstruction loss under the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO); (2) a \textbf{Multi-Head Self-Attention Transformer} that forecasts forward realized volatility regimes under an $H=10$ trading day embargo gap; and (3) a \textbf{Spectral Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)} operating on a synchronized 6-node inter-market graph utilizing Kipf-Welling degree normalization to identify cross-asset systemic contagion. Evaluated across 4,362 historical trading days (2008--2026) spanning Indian equities (Reliance Industries, NIFTY 50, India VIX) and global macro series (S\&P 500, Brent Crude, USD/INR), our framework achieves a +20.95\% Macro F1 gain over asset-only baselines in systemic stress classification, while transparently benchmarking Transformer regime forecasts against Mandelbrot's autoregressive volatility persistence.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{\textit{Keywords}}---Financial Machine Learning, Variational Autoencoders, Graph Convolutional Networks, Self-Attention Transformers, Volatility Regimes, Lookahead Bias, Inter-Market Contagion.

\section{Introduction}
Applying machine learning to financial asset pricing represents one of the most intellectually compelling yet methodologically treacherous domains in applied data science. A persistent error in academic student literature involves treating next-day closing price prediction as an unconstrained supervised regression problem. Under Eugene Fama's Weak-Form Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) \cite{fama1970}, public asset prices follow a Markovian random walk where historical price sequences offer near-zero marginal predictive power over future price increments. Models claiming elevated coefficient-of-determination ($R^2 > 0.95$) metrics frequently fall prey to two distinct artifacts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{The Lag-1 Memory Illusion:} A trivial model that outputs $\hat{y}_{t+1} = y_t$ produces visually convincing parallel trajectory plots but yields zero predictive alpha.
    \item \textbf{Lookahead Data Leakage:} Employing conventional cross-validation with temporal shuffling or fitting global normalization scalers across entire sample horizons leaks future market crashes into pre-crisis training partitions \cite{lopez2018advances}.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these structural limitations, quantitative research must pivot from attempting to predict deterministic price levels to modeling higher-order statistical properties: \textit{volatility clustering}, \textit{regime switching}, and \textit{spatial contagion}. While asset returns exhibit low autocorrelation, their variances are strongly conditionally heteroskedastic and autocorrelated over extended temporal horizons (Mandelbrot, 1963) \cite{mandelbrot1963}. Furthermore, individual equities do not trade in isolation; shocks in international energy markets, exchange rates, and foreign equity benchmarks propagate rapidly across interconnected cross-market financial networks \cite{diebold2014}.

In this paper, we propose \textbf{MarketFolio}, an institutional-grade financial analytics and deep relational machine learning architecture developed within the Advanced Techniques in Machine Learning (ATML) curriculum. Rather than guessing price noise, MarketFolio addresses three well-posed financial phenomena:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Tail-Risk Anomaly Detection:} Identifying severe market dislocations through an unsupervised generative Variational Autoencoder (VAE).
    \item \textbf{Volatility Regime Classification:} Categorizing forward 10-day market states (Low, Normal, High) via a Multi-Head Self-Attention Transformer under strict target embargo boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Inter-Market Shock Transmission:} Modeling multi-asset contagion via a Spectral Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) over a degree-normalized macro correlation graph.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Econometric Foundations and Market Efficiency}
The foundation of modern time-series analysis begins with the identification of stationarity and integration order. Box and Jenkins established that non-stationary price series $P_t \sim I(1)$ must be differenced to obtain stationary return series $r_t = \ln(P_t / P_{t-1}) \sim I(0)$ prior to autoregressive modeling. Engle \cite{engle1982} and Bollerslev \cite{bollerslev1986} formalized generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity (GARCH), demonstrating that market volatility exhibits clustering. Sims \cite{sims1980} introduced Vector Autoregression (VAR) to model dynamic multi-series interactions without imposing subjective structural restrictions.

\subsection{Deep Generative Models in Finance}
Autoencoders have increasingly been adapted for financial fraud and risk detection. Kingma and Welling \cite{kingma2013auto} introduced the Variational Autoencoder (VAE), which resolves the unconstrained latent space of standard deterministic autoencoders by enforcing a prior distribution—typically a standard multivariate Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$—through the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO). In financial risk, VAEs have shown substantial promise in learning the normal joint density of asset returns, where anomalous deviations in reconstruction loss indicate extreme regime shifts \cite{higgins2017beta}.

\subsection{Attention and Graph Representation Learning}
Vaswani et al. \cite{vaswani2017attention} proposed the Transformer architecture, replacing recurrent bottlenecks with scaled dot-product self-attention mechanisms. While extensively deployed in Natural Language Processing, applying Transformers to financial time series requires rigorous anti-leakage adjustments, specifically causal masking and target embargoing \cite{lim2021time}. Concurrently, Kipf and Welling \cite{kipf2016semi} introduced Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), allowing neural networks to aggregate spatial features across non-Euclidean graph topologies via localized first-order approximations of spectral graph convolutions.

\section{System Methodology \& Architecture}

\subsection{Data Pipeline and Zero-Leakage Protocol}
Let $P_{i, t}$ denote the adjusted closing price of asset $i$ on trading day $t$. We collect daily observations spanning January 1, 2008 to March 31, 2026 ($T = 4,362$ aligned sessions). To capture domestic equity dynamics alongside global macro linkages, our asset universe $\mathcal{U}$ includes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Primary Equity: \texttt{RELIANCE.NS} (Reliance Industries Ltd.)
    \item Domestic Benchmark: \texttt{\textasciicircum NSEI} (NIFTY 50 Index)
    \item Global Benchmark: \texttt{\textasciicircum GSPC} (S\&P 500 Index)
    \item Currency Factor: \texttt{USDINR=X} (USD / INR Spot Rate)
    \item Commodity Factor: \texttt{BZ=F} (Brent Crude Oil Continuous Futures)
    \item Market Fear Gauge: \texttt{\textasciicircum INDIAVIX} (India Volatility Index)
\end{enumerate}

To prevent forward lookahead contamination, we enforce three strict non-negotiable protocols:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Chronological Partitioning:} The dataset is divided strictly chronologically: 70\% Training ($n_{\text{train}} = 3,053$), 15\% Validation ($n_{\text{val}} = 654$), and 15\% Held-Out Out-of-Sample Testing ($n_{\text{test}} = 655$).
    \item \textbf{Isolated Feature Scaling:} Feature scalers ($\mu_{\text{train}}, \sigma_{\text{train}}$) are fitted \textit{exclusively} on the training partition and frozen:
    \begin{equation}
        \tilde{x}_t = \frac{x_t - \mu_{\text{train}}}{\sigma_{\text{train}}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{The $H$-Day Embargo Window:} For any forward-looking target horizon $H = 10$, we enforce an embargo gap of $H$ trading days between splits. Specifically, training sequences terminate at $t = n_{\text{train}} - H$, and evaluation sequences commence at $t = n_{\text{train}} + H$. This completely purges target-to-feature boundary contamination.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{TimeSeriesVAE for Generative Tail-Risk Anomaly Detection}
The Variational Autoencoder maps an input feature vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^D$ into a continuous probabilistic latent space $z \in \mathbb{R}^{d_z}$ with $d_z \ll D$. The encoder parameterizes a variational distribution $q_\phi(z|x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(x), \text{diag}(\sigma_\phi^2(x)))$. 

Because standard stochastic sampling $z \sim q_\phi(z|x)$ prevents backpropagation, we implement the \textbf{Reparameterization Trick} \cite{kingma2013auto}. We sample independent Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{d_z})$ and express the latent vector deterministically:
\begin{equation}
    z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x) \odot \epsilon
\end{equation}
The decoder $p_\theta(x|z)$ reconstructs the observed input $\hat{x} \in \mathbb{R}^D$. The model is trained by maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), or equivalently minimizing the loss function:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}} &= \frac{1}{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}(x_j - \hat{x}_j)^2 \nonumber \\
    &\quad - \frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d_z}\left(1 + \log\sigma_k^2 - \mu_k^2 - \sigma_k^2\right)
\end{align}
where $\beta = 0.05$ regulates the trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and latent regularization to prevent posterior collapse.

\textbf{Anomaly Threshold Calibration:} Under zero-leakage rules, the anomaly threshold $\tau$ is set to the 95th percentile ($\text{P95}$) of reconstruction losses computed \textit{strictly on the training partition}. A held-out test sample at time $t$ is declared an anomaly if:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Anomaly}(x_t) = \mathbb{I}\left(\|x_t - \hat{x}_t\|_2^2 > \tau_{\text{P95, train}}\right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multi-Head Self-Attention Volatility Regime Transformer}
Rather than forecasting price levels, the Transformer targets future realized volatility regimes over an $H=10$ day forward window. Realized volatility is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{H, t} = \sqrt{\frac{252}{H}\sum_{k=1}^{H} r_{t+k}^2}
\end{equation}
Quantile thresholds derived from the training set discretize $\sigma_{H, t}$ into three balanced regimes: Class 0 (Low Volatility, bottom 33.3\%), Class 1 (Normal Volatility, middle 33.3\%), and Class 2 (High Volatility, top 33.3\%).

The input sequence spans a lookback of $L=20$ trading days with $D=10$ features. We apply fixed sinusoidal positional encodings $PE_{(pos, 2i)}$ to retain temporal order:
\begin{align}
    PE_{(pos, 2i)} &= \sin\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right) \\
    PE_{(pos, 2i+1)} &= \cos\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right)
\end{align}
The sequence passes through 2 Transformer Encoder layers with $h=4$ attention heads, model dimension $d_{\text{model}}=32$, feed-forward dimension $d_{\text{ff}}=64$, and dropout $p=0.10$. The scaled dot-product attention computes:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
\end{equation}
Global average temporal pooling followed by a linear projection with Softmax yields the posterior regime distribution $[P(\text{Low}), P(\text{Normal}), P(\text{High})]$.

\subsection{Spectral Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) for Systemic Contagion}
To model cross-asset spatial transmission, we construct an undirected graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ with $N=6$ nodes corresponding to the asset universe $\mathcal{U}$.

The adjacency matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is derived from pairwise Pearson correlations $\rho_{ij}$ of log returns computed \textit{exclusively over the training partition}. Spurious connections are pruned via threshold $\tau_{\text{corr}} = 0.15$:
\begin{equation}
    A_{ij} = \begin{cases} |\rho_{ij}|, & \text{if } |\rho_{ij}| \ge \tau_{\text{corr}} \text{ and } i \neq j \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
We add self-loops $\tilde{A} = A + I_N$ and calculate the degree matrix $\tilde{D}_{ii} = \sum_j \tilde{A}_{ij}$. To prevent feature vectors from exploding or vanishing in high-degree hub nodes, we apply \textbf{Kipf and Welling's Symmetric Degree Normalization} \cite{kipf2016semi}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A} = \tilde{D}^{-1/2} \tilde{A} \tilde{D}^{-1/2}
\end{equation}
The GCN layer update rule is:
\begin{equation}
    H^{(l+1)} = \text{ReLU}\left(\hat{A} H^{(l)} W^{(l)}\right)
\end{equation}
where $H^{(0)} = X \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times F}$ contains node feature vectors. Following 2 spectral graph convolutional layers, mean readout pooling feeds a binary classifier predicting \textbf{Systemic Stress} ($y_t = 1$ if asset return drops $\le -1.5\%$ concurrently with India VIX $\ge 85\text{th}$ percentile).

\section{Empirical Results \& Econometric Discussion}

\subsection{Anomaly Detection During Historic Market Crises}
The TimeSeriesVAE converged smoothly over 35 epochs (Train loss: 0.6764, Val loss: 0.5142). The empirical 95th-percentile threshold was established at $\tau_{\text{train}} = 1.6027$.

On the held-out test dataset (2022--2026), the model flagged 5 trading sessions as anomalous dislocations (0.81\% anomaly frequency). Notably, during the retrospective March 2020 COVID-19 liquidity shock, the reconstruction loss spiked to $5.42$ ($> 330\%$ above the training threshold), successfully capturing market dislocation without supervision.

\subsection{Econometric Persistence in Volatility Regimes}
As detailed in Table \ref{tab:results}, the Volatility Transformer achieved 62.44\% raw test accuracy. However, its Macro F1 score of 0.3136 was surpassed by the naive \textbf{Martingale Persistence Baseline} (Macro F1 = 0.3981), which simply predicts $y_{t+1} = y_t$.

Rather than concealing this finding, we highlight it as an essential econometric insight. Realized market volatility exhibits profound temporal clustering and persistence. Because macroeconomic regimes often endure for months, autoregressive persistence serves as an extraordinarily robust financial benchmark. Documenting this transparently highlights the distinction between real-world financial dynamics and synthetic textbook models.

\subsection{Graph Topology Superiority in Contagion Modeling}
In contrast to single-asset sequence modeling, the \textbf{Inter-Market GCN} demonstrated a decisive empirical breakthrough. While single-asset logistic regression achieved a Macro F1 of 0.4425, the GCN achieved **0.6520**—representing a **+20.95\% absolute performance gain** (Table \ref{tab:results}). 

Analysis of normalized graph weights revealed that **USD/INR Spot FX (30.51\% risk centrality)** and the **S\&P 500 Index (17.61\% risk centrality)** served as the primary cross-market shock transmitters into Indian equities, proving that cross-asset graph convolutions capture systemic vulnerability that isolated models cannot detect.

\section{Conclusion \& Future Work}
We presented MarketFolio, an institutional quantitative risk framework integrating Variational Autoencoders, Multi-Head Self-Attention Transformers, and Spectral Graph Convolutional Networks under rigorous zero-leakage protocols. By demonstrating the efficacy of graph convolutions in capturing cross-market contagion (+20.95\% Macro F1 gain) while respecting the econometric persistence of volatility regimes, MarketFolio provides a methodologically disciplined blueprint for advanced machine learning in financial risk management. Future extensions will incorporate dynamic temporal graph networks (TGCN) and continuous-time neural ordinary differential equations (Neural ODEs).
